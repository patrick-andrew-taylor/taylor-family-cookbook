A big-batch pantry tuna salad — no chopping required. The whole thing is built
from canned tuna, mayo, and the spice cabinet: celery seed stands in for celery,
onion powder for onion, and dried dill for the pickles. Makes enough for 6 to 8
sandwiches and keeps in the fridge for 3 to 4 days.

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|:-:|:-:|
| Chunk White Albacore Tuna in Water | 4 Cans (5 Ounces Each), Drained Well |
| Mayonnaise | 3/4 Cup, Plus More to Taste |
| Dried Dill | 1 Teaspoon |
| Onion Powder | 3/4 Teaspoon |
| Garlic Powder | 1/2 Teaspoon |
| Celery Seed | 1/2 Teaspoon |
| Ground Mustard | 1/2 Teaspoon |
| Smoked Paprika | 1/4 Teaspoon |
| Cayenne Pepper | 1 Pinch, Optional |
| Kosher Salt | To Taste[^1] |
| Black Pepper | To Taste, Freshly Ground |

## Instructions

1. Drain the tuna thoroughly — press each lid down into the can and squeeze out as much water as you can. Watery tuna makes watery salad.
2. In a small bowl, stir the dill, onion powder, garlic powder, celery seed, ground mustard, smoked paprika, and cayenne into the mayonnaise until smooth.
3. Flake the tuna into a large bowl, breaking up any big chunks with a fork.
4. Fold the seasoned mayo into the tuna until evenly coated. Add another spoonful of mayo if it looks dry.
5. Refrigerate for at least 30 minutes — the dried spices need the time to wake up, and the salad tastes noticeably better for it.
6. Taste and season with salt and pepper, adding more of any spice that got lost along the way.[^2]

> Serve on toasted bread, on crackers, or straight off the fork over greens.

[^1]: Go easy at first — canned tuna and mayo both bring their own salt.
[^2]: If anything sharp is within reach — a squeeze of lemon, a splash of pickle juice, or a small spoonful of vinegar — stir it in at the end. It's optional, but it brightens the whole batch.
