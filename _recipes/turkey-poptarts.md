Turkey pot pie, handheld. A thick, chilled pot pie filling sealed inside pie crust rectangles, baked until golden, then finished with a gravy glaze and crushed fried onions right where the frosting and sprinkles would go.

Great for Thanksgiving leftovers, and they freeze beautifully.[^1] One package of pie crust makes six; for a full dozen, use two packages and double everything.

## Ingredients

### Filling Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|:-:|:-:|
| Unsalted Butter | 2 Tbsp |
| Yellow Onion | 1/2 Small, Finely Diced |
| Carrot | 1 Small, Finely Diced |
| Celery | 1 Stalk, Finely Diced |
| Garlic | 1 Clove, Minced |
| Flour | 2 Tbsp |
| Turkey or Chicken Stock | 1/2 Cup |
| Whole Milk | 2 Tbsp |
| Cooked Turkey[^2] | 1 Cup, Finely Chopped |
| Frozen Peas | 1/4 Cup |
| Fresh Thyme | 1 tsp, Chopped |
| Poultry Seasoning | 1/4 tsp |
| Salt | To Taste |
| Black Pepper | To Taste |

### Pastry Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|:-:|:-:|
| Refrigerated Pie Crusts[^3] | 1 Package / 2 Crusts |
| Egg | 1 (For Eggwash) |
| Flour | For Dusting |

### Topping Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|:-:|:-:|
| Turkey Gravy[^4] | 1/2 Cup |
| French Fried Onions | 1/2 Cup, Lightly Crushed |
| Fresh Parsley | 1 Tbsp, Chopped |

## Instructions

### Filling Instructions

1. Melt the butter in a large skillet over medium heat.
2. Add the onion, carrot, and celery, and cook until soft and translucent, 6-8 minutes.
3. Add the garlic, thyme, and poultry seasoning, and cook 1 minute more.
4. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir constantly for 2 minutes to cook out the raw flour taste.
5. Gradually whisk in the stock, then the milk, and simmer until very thick — thicker than you would want a pot pie, closer to a paste.[^5]
6. Stir in the turkey and peas, and season aggressively with salt and pepper.[^6]
7. Spread the filling onto a plate or sheet pan in a thin layer and refrigerate until completely cold, at least 1 hour.[^7]

### Assembly Instructions

1. Heat the oven to 400&deg;F (200&deg;C), and line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
2. On a lightly floured surface, roll each pie crust a little thinner than it comes, to about 1/8 inch.
3. Cut 3x4 inch rectangles from each crust, gathering and re-rolling the scraps once — you want twelve total, six tops and six bottoms.[^8]
4. Lay the six bottoms on the lined baking sheet, and beat the egg with a splash of water for the eggwash.
5. Mound a heaping 1/4 cup of cold filling on each bottom, leaving a 1/2 inch border all the way around.[^9]
6. Brush the border with eggwash, lay a top rectangle over each, and press down from the center out to push out any air.
7. Seal the edges by pressing all the way around with the tines of a fork.
8. Cut three small vents in the top of each tart with the tip of a knife.[^10]
9. Chill the assembled tarts for 15 minutes, then brush the tops with eggwash.

### Baking Instructions

1. Bake for 22-25 minutes, until deep golden brown and the edges are crisp.
2. Rest on the pan for 5 minutes, then move to a rack.[^11]

### Topping Instructions

1. Warm the gravy in a small pan until it is thick enough to coat the back of a spoon but still pourable.
2. Spoon a stripe of gravy down the center of each tart, leaving the fork-crimped border bare so it still reads as a poptart.
3. Scatter the crushed fried onions and parsley over the gravy while it is still wet.

> Serve warm, with extra gravy for dipping!

[^1]: Freeze them assembled but unbaked — on a sheet pan until solid, then into a bag. Bake straight from frozen, adding 8-10 minutes. Do not put these in an actual toaster.
[^2]: Leftover roast turkey is ideal, dark meat especially. Rotisserie chicken works the other eleven months of the year.
[^3]: Homemade all-butter pie dough is better if you have it; you need two discs. Puff pastry also works, but it puffs the tops into pillows instead of staying flat and poptart-like.
[^4]: Leftover gravy is best. Jarred gravy is fine, and pan drippings loosened with stock are better than both.
[^5]: This is the whole trick. A loose filling steams, blows out the seams, and leaves you with soggy bottoms.
[^6]: Cold food tastes flatter than hot food, and this filling goes into the pastry cold — season it past where it seems right.
[^7]: Overnight is fine, and makes assembly much easier. Do not skip this step.
[^8]: A package of two crusts is just barely enough dough for six tarts, so cut in a grid and keep the scraps. Expect a little filling left over — it is very good on toast.
[^9]: Pack it flat rather than piling it up; a tall mound will not seal.
[^10]: The vents matter more than they look. Without them the tarts split at the seams.
[^11]: Straight onto the rack, or the bottoms sweat and soften.
